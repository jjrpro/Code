# Co-Owned Services Venture — Launch Playbook (50/50, NY)

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Setup:** Brand-new, **services / done-for-you** business · **50/50 co-owned** · separate from
JR's existing LLC businesses · plan = start now, form a multi-member LLC soon.

> Not legal or tax advice — general info, NY-based. The 50/50 agreement and the LLC filing are
> worth a one-time attorney/CPA review; this playbook gets you organized and 90% of the way there.

---

## The key fact that resolves "start now vs. LLC"

In NY, an LLC **exists and can operate the day you file the Articles of Organization**
(~$200, often same-day online). The pricey **newspaper publication requirement has a 120-day
window you complete AFTER** — it's a follow-up, not a gate.

➡️ **You can file the LLC this week, take clients under it immediately, and handle publication
within 120 days.** "Start now" and "form the LLC" are the same move.

**Why go straight to the LLC here (vs. running as a bare partnership):** a *services* business
with a *50/50 partner* is the highest-liability setup to run unincorporated — in a general
partnership you're personally liable for your own work **and** your partner's mistakes (joint
& several). The LLC shield matters more here than for a solo info product.

---

## Priority #1 — the 50/50 agreement (do this before clients/money)

Two equal owners is the classic deadlock trap. Put it in writing — as a partnership agreement
now, which converts directly into the **LLC operating agreement**. Must-have clauses:

| Clause | Why it matters at 50/50 |
|---|---|
| **Deadlock breaker** | Two equal votes can freeze the business. Split *final say by domain* (one owns sales, one owns delivery/ops) or name a neutral tiebreaker. Never 50/50 on everything. |
| **Buy-sell / exit** | What happens if a partner leaves, checks out, or dies. Valuation method + buyout terms + first right of refusal. Most-skipped, most-regretted clause. |
| **Capital contributions** | Who's putting in what — cash, equipment, existing clients, sweat. Document it precisely even if "equal." |
| **Profit/loss split + draws** | 50/50 split; define *when* and *how much* each of you can take out (draw schedule) vs. leave in. |
| **Roles & responsibilities** | Who does sales, delivery, books, admin. Prevents "I thought you were doing that." |
| **Spending authority** | What either partner can spend solo vs. what needs both signatures (e.g., >$500 = both). |
| **IP & client ownership** | The *business* owns clients, contracts, and work product — not either individual. Critical for a services firm. |
| **Non-solicit / non-compete** | While partners and for a period after exit — protects the client base. |
| **Dispute resolution** | Mediation/arbitration step before anyone lawyers up. |

## The launch checklist (order of operations)

1. **Write the 50/50 agreement** (clauses above). Even a 2-page version beats nothing; upgrade
   with an attorney before it's big.
2. **File NY LLC** — Articles of Organization (~$200, online). Multi-member, both owners.
   - LLC is live to operate immediately.
   - Calendar the **publication requirement (within 120 days)** — two newspapers, county-assigned,
     ~$1–2k in the NYC/SI area. Budget for it; it's not optional but it's not urgent-day-one.
3. **Get the EIN** (free, IRS, ~10 min) — a multi-member entity needs its own EIN; never run a
   2-owner business on a personal SSN.
4. **Open a joint business bank account** (both names, using the EIN). All revenue/expenses flow
   through it — clean books from dollar one, and both owners visible.
5. **Operating agreement** = formalize the 50/50 agreement under the LLC (same terms).
6. **Client service agreement / contract template** — scope, deliverables, payment terms,
   limitation of liability, kill/cancellation terms. Don't do handshake gigs (see liability below).
7. **Payment rail** — Stripe (invoices + payment links) or Square, under the LLC + EIN + joint
   account. B2B can also just be ACH/Zelle to the business account.

## Services-specific liability (your real exposure)

A services business gets sued for the *work*, not just for debts. Until and even after the LLC:

- **Get business insurance** — General Liability, plus **Professional Liability / E&O** if you're
  advising or delivering expert work. Cheap (often $40–100/mo) and it's your front-line protection.
- **Always use a written client contract** with a **limitation-of-liability clause** and clear
  scope. This caps what a client can come after you for.
- **Check licensing** — some NY services require an occupational/professional license. Confirm
  for your specific service before taking money.
- **Sales tax** — many services aren't taxable in NY, but some are (certain repair, maintenance,
  installation, info services). Confirm whether yours is; if so, register for a Certificate of
  Authority and collect.

---

## Taxes — no disruption

A 50/50 general partnership and a 2-member LLC are **taxed identically**: pass-through, the
business files **Form 1065**, each owner gets a **K-1** and reports their half on their personal
return. Forming the LLC **changes nothing about your taxes** — it only adds the liability shield.
(Later, once profitable, a CPA may suggest an **S-corp election** to cut self-employment tax —
revisit at ~$80–100k+ net.)
