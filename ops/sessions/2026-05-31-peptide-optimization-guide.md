# Peptide Optimization & Safety Guide — Semax + Retatrutide + Tesamorelin

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Summary:** A harm-reduction, evidence-graded guide for getting the best (and safest)
results from a stack of Semax (~300 mcg / 10u daily), Retatrutide (~40u weekly), and
Tesamorelin (~1 mg / 10u daily), all reconstituted with bacteriostatic water. Built from
5 parallel web-research passes with confidence flags and citations.

> **Read this first.** This is educational harm-reduction info for someone who has already
> decided to use these compounds — not medical advice and not an endorsement to start.
> Three honest headline facts shape everything below:
> 1. **Retatrutide is investigational** — not FDA-approved anywhere as of 2026 (Phase 3).
> 2. **Tesamorelin is FDA-approved only for HIV-related fat redistribution** — your use is off-label.
> 3. **Semax is not FDA-approved** in the West at all (Russian-market drug).
>
> **None of these three has ever been studied together.** Every "stacking" statement here
> is mechanistic reasoning, clearly flagged, not trial data. The single highest-leverage
> move you can make is getting **baseline bloodwork and a doctor in the loop** — even a
> telehealth/longevity clinic. Everything else is optimization around the edges.

## 0. Your actual doses (confirmed from your reconstitutions, 2026-05-31)

All three are mixed to the **same concentration: 10 mg/mL → 1 unit = 0.1 mg (100 mcg)** across
every vial. Easy mental model: **every unit = 0.1 mg.**

| Peptide | Mix | Concentration | Per unit | **Your dose** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Retatrutide** | 30 mg → 300u (3 mL) | 10 mg/mL | 0.1 mg | 40u = **4 mg/week** |
| **Semax** | 20 mg → 200u (2 mL) | 10 mg/mL | 0.1 mg (100 mcg) | 10u = **1,000 mcg/day** |
| **Tesamorelin** | 20 mg → 200u (2 mL) | 10 mg/mL | 0.1 mg | 10u = **1 mg/day** |

- **Retatrutide 4 mg/week** = step 2 of the 1→2→4→8→12 ladder. Legit, but expect stronger
  appetite suppression, more GI, bigger HR bump, and faster loss than 2 mg. If you jumped
  straight to 4 (vs titrating from 2), that's why GI may be rough — hold here, don't climb.
- **Semax 1,000 mcg/day** is ~2–5× the typical 200–600 mcg nootropic range. Wide safety margin,
  but likely no extra benefit past ~300–600 mcg and more overstim/sleep risk. 3 units (300 mcg)
  would hit the studied range and stretch the vial 3×.
- **Tesamorelin 1 mg/day** is below the 2 mg approved dose — milder effects + milder sides.
  Conservative; 20u would be the full trial-strength 2 mg if you ever wanted it (check IGF-1 first).

## The two things that matter most (if you read nothing else)

1. **Recompute your dose mg any time you change vial size or water volume.** Units measure
   volume, not drug. Your current setup = 0.1 mg/unit everywhere; if a future vial or water
   amount differs, the mg/unit changes and a wrong assumption can mean a 2–4× over/under-dose.
   Write the mg/unit on each vial. See §2.
2. **Protect your muscle.** Retatrutide drives fast, deep weight loss, and ~25–40% of weight
   lost on this drug class is lean mass. **High protein (1.6–2.2 g/kg) + resistance training
   2–4×/week** is the whole game. Without it you'll lose muscle along with the fat. See §6.

## 2. Retatrutide — the heavy hitter (and the riskiest to dose)

**What it does:** A once-weekly single molecule hitting three receptors. The extra
**glucagon** arm (vs semaglutide/tirzepatide) adds energy expenditure + liver-fat burning,
which is why it produces the biggest weight loss of the class. *(High confidence)*

**Efficacy (Phase 2, NEJM 2023, n=338, 48 wks):** −8.7% (1mg), −17.1% (4mg), −22.8% (8mg),
**−24.2% (12mg)** vs −2.1% placebo. Phase 3 topline (2026) up to ~28–30%. Weight was still
dropping at 48 weeks — no plateau yet. *(High confidence)*
[NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2301972) ·
[Lilly](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-phase-2-retatrutide-results-published-new-england-journal)

### ⚠️ THE DOSE MATH (most safety-critical thing in this whole document)

Insulin syringes are **U-100: 100 units = 1 mL, so 1 unit = 0.01 mL — always.** Units measure
**volume, not drug**. The mg depends on your reconstitution:

> **Concentration (mg/mL) = vial mg ÷ mL of water added**
> **mg per unit = concentration ÷ 100**

**Your mix:** 30 mg vial → 300 units (3 mL) BAC water = 10 mg/mL = **0.1 mg/unit**, so
**40 units = 4 mg/week** (and your weekly injection volume is 0.4 mL — inject slowly).

For reference, how the same 40-unit mark changes with different mixes of a 30 mg vial:

| Water added | Concentration | mg per unit | **40 units =** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3 mL (300u)** | **10 mg/mL** | **0.10 mg** | **4 mg** ← your dose |
| 6 mL (600u) | 5 mg/mL | 0.05 mg | 2 mg |
| 1.5 mL (150u) | 20 mg/mL | 0.20 mg | 8 mg ⚠️ |

**Action:** Any time you open a new vial or change the water volume, recompute and
**write the mg/unit on the vial in marker.**
Most overdoses come from (a) wrong water volume, (b) wrong vial strength, (c) confusing
units with mL, (d) a U-40 vs U-100 syringe mismatch. *(High confidence — arithmetic)*

### Titration (slower = fewer side effects, zero efficacy penalty)
Trial schedule: **2 → 4 → 8 → 12 mg**, ≥4 weeks per step. The trial itself proved a **2 mg
start beats a 4 mg start** for GI tolerability. If nausea hits, **hold at your current dose
longer** rather than pushing up. There's no prize for climbing fast. *(High confidence)*

### Side effects
- **GI (common, dose-related, worst during dose increases):** nausea, vomiting, diarrhea,
  constipation. Nausea ran ~14% (low dose) to ~60% (12 mg). Settles as you stabilize. *(High)*
- **Heart rate ↑ — the standout signal:** dose-dependent **+5 to +8 bpm** resting HR,
  peaking ~week 24 then partially easing. Driven by the glucagon arm. **Track resting HR;**
  palpitations or a big sustained rise = see a doctor. *(High on direction/magnitude)*
- **Pancreatitis** (rare, ~0.4%), **gallbladder** issues (low single digits; rapid weight
  loss is itself a gallstone risk — see §6), **hypoglycemia** (low solo, but real if combined
  with insulin/sulfonylureas), **thyroid C-cell** class concern (boxed warning on approved
  GLP-1s; contraindicated with personal/family history of medullary thyroid cancer / MEN2). *(High)*

### Lean mass
DXA substudy: **~35% of weight lost was lean mass** — "not worse than other GLP-1s" but NOT
muscle-sparing. Bigger total loss = bigger absolute muscle loss. Mitigation in §6. Ignore any
vendor "retatrutide for bulking/recomp" claims — pure marketing, zero trial support. *(High)*

## 3. Tesamorelin — visceral fat + GH/recovery

**What it does:** Stimulates your pituitary to release **your own GH in natural pulses**,
raising IGF-1, which preferentially burns **visceral (deep belly) fat**. FDA-approved at
**2 mg/day**; **your 1 mg (20 mg → 200u → 10u) is half that / sub-therapeutic** — expect
milder effects *and* milder side effects than the trials. 20u would be the full 2 mg if you
ever want it (check IGF-1 first). *(High confidence)*
[FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/022505s020lbl.pdf) ·
[NEJM Falutz 2007](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa072375)

**Results & timeline (trial data at 2 mg):** ~15–18% visceral fat reduction at 26 weeks;
IGF-1 detectably up by ~week 13; triglycerides/cholesterol improve; liver fat drops. It
targets **visceral fat, not scale weight or subcutaneous fat**, over 2–6 months. Effects
**reverse off-drug** (visceral fat creeps back) — it's a maintenance tool. *(High)*

### Optimal timing — this is where you can actually optimize
- **Dose at night, ~30–90 min before bed.** Your biggest natural GH pulse fires at the onset
  of deep (slow-wave) sleep; bedtime dosing **rides that pulse instead of fighting it.** *(High on physiology)*
- **On an empty stomach — finish eating ≥2 h before.** Food → insulin, and **insulin blunts
  GH release.** Eating too close to your dose literally wastes it. Keep dinner earlier and not
  heavily carb-loaded. *(High on mechanism)*
- Lift earlier in the day if you want — don't compromise the fasted-before-bed window to try
  to "stack GH around a workout" (that's lore, not evidence). *(Timing rationale: high)*

### Side effects & the glucose catch
- Joint pain (arthralgia), **edema / water retention**, carpal-tunnel-type symptoms (~2%),
  injection-site reactions. These are classic GH-excess signs — a clue you may be dosing high. *(High)*
- **GH is diabetogenic** — it nudges blood sugar **up**. In trials the net effect was small
  (HbA1c +0.12%, often transient), but it's real and matters because you're *also* on
  retatrutide (see §5). *(High)*

### Reconstitution reality check (important, honest)
The licensed product is mixed with **sterile water and injected immediately** — the label
says **do not store after mixing**, because tesamorelin is **fragile and aggregates
irreversibly above 8 °C within hours**. Multi-dose BAC-water vials (what you're doing) are
standard in the peptide world but **depart from validated stability data.** Community
consensus is "good ~3–4 weeks refrigerated," but treat that as **folklore, not fact.**
Safest assumption: **keep it cold, dark, and fresh; use it promptly.** *(High on fragility;
the 3–4 week window is low-confidence community lore)*

## 4. Semax — the cognitive piece (lowest-evidence, lowest-risk)

**What it does:** Best-supported mechanism is **BDNF/NGF/TrkB upregulation** (neurotrophic,
neuroprotective) — robust in *rats*, only inferred in humans. Also indirect dopaminergic/
serotonergic effects and enkephalinase inhibition (mood). The honest caveat: nearly all human
data are **small, Russian, and rarely replicated** to Western standards. *(Mechanism in
animals: high. Human efficacy: medium-low.)*
[Dolotov 2006, J Neurochem](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1471-4159.2006.03658.x) ·
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semax)

### Optimization
- **Route:** Intranasal is the original, studied route and the more defensible one for a
  *brain* target (partial direct nose-to-brain transport). SubQ is a biohacker adaptation that
  **loses** the nose-to-brain advantage — "subQ is better" is preference, not evidence. *(Medium)*
- **Half-life is minutes** (~2–5 min) but effects last hours-to-~a-day via gene-expression
  changes and the active Pro-Gly-Pro metabolite. *(High on half-life)*
- **Timing:** Morning / early afternoon, **last dose by ~3–4 PM** so it doesn't disrupt sleep.
  Dosing before a cognitively demanding block is the common practice and roughly fits the rat
  time-course (effects build over ~1.5–8 h). *(Medium)*
- **Your 1,000 mcg/day** (20 mg → 200u → 0.1 mg/unit → 10u = 1 mg) is **~2–5× the commonly
  cited 200–600 mcg range.** Wide safety margin so not a danger flag, but likely no added
  benefit past ~300–600 mcg, more overstim/anxiety/sleep risk, and ~3× the peptide burned.
  **3 units (300 mcg)** would hit the studied range and stretch the vial 3×; or split AM +
  midday. Your call — just know you're on the high end. *(High on math)*
- **Tolerance/cycling:** No documented tolerance, but long-term data are thin. The "cycle to
  preserve receptor sensitivity" rule is **bro-science** — cycling (e.g. 4–8 wks on / 2–4 off)
  is defensible only as general caution given limited long-term safety data. *(Low on the stated rationale)*

### Interactions
- **Stimulants** (caffeine, Adderall, modafinil): additive — possible overstimulation/anxiety/
  BP. Worth caution. **SSRIs/MAOIs:** theoretical only, low risk (no case reports); MAOI
  avoidance is precautionary. *(Medium)*
- **No known metabolic interaction with retatrutide or tesamorelin** — it doesn't touch
  incretin, GH, or glucose pathways. It's the "free" addition to the stack pharmacologically;
  the real caveat is just that its *benefits* are less proven. *(Medium)*

## 5. How the three interact (all mechanistic — no human studies exist on this combo)

- **The glucose tug-of-war:** Tesamorelin (via GH) pushes glucose **up**; retatrutide pushes it
  **down** hard (and improves HbA1c). **Net direction is almost certainly improved glucose** —
  retatrutide's effect dwarfs tesamorelin's mild bump. But retatrutide's glucagon arm also
  raises glucose somewhat, so the net is **not perfectly predictable → monitor fasting glucose
  + HbA1c.** *(Mechanism: high. This specific combo: medium — inference, not data.)*
- **Possible muscle tailwind:** Tesamorelin's GH/IGF-1 rise is *plausibly* muscle-protective
  during retatrutide's aggressive deficit — a real reason this pairing *might* spare lean mass
  better than retatrutide alone. **No trial tests this. Treat as theory, not a guarantee.** *(Low)*
- **Semax:** no pharmacologic conflict with either. *(Medium)*
- **Don't over-dose tesamorelin chasing synergy** — pushing IGF-1 supraphysiologic brings
  insulin resistance, edema, and joint issues, and partially fights retatrutide's glucose win.
  Keep IGF-1 upper-normal, not above range. *(Medium)*

## 6. Diet & training — where you actually win or lose (HIGHEST LEVERAGE)

### Protein (non-negotiable)
- **Target 1.6–2.2 g/kg bodyweight/day.** In a deficit, the **higher end is protective**.
  Below ~1.0–1.2 g/kg, muscle-loss risk climbs sharply. *(High)*
  [Examine](https://examine.com/guides/protein-intake/)
- **Eating enough when appetite is gone** (the core challenge on retatrutide):
  - **Protein FIRST** at every meal — fill limited stomach space with the priority macro.
  - **Front-load early in the day** — appetite is best in the morning, most suppressed later.
  - **Eat on a schedule, not on hunger cues** (cues are unreliable on incretins).
  - **25–30 g protein per "occasion," 4–5 occasions/day.** Snacks under ~10 g are just calories.
  - Easy options when queasy: whey shakes, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, bone broth, tuna. *(Medium — RD guidance)*
- Then prioritize **fiber → micronutrients** (low food volume risks deficiencies). *(High)*

### Resistance training (the other half of muscle preservation)
- **Lift 3–4×/week**, hitting each major muscle **~2–3×/week**, compound-focused (squat, hinge,
  press, pull), progressive overload. **Resistance training — not cardio — is what spares
  muscle in a deficit** (prevented ~93% of diet-induced muscle loss in one trial). *(High)*
  [RT meta-analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5946208/)
- Add light cardio/steps for heart health and mood, but not at the expense of lifting/recovery. *(Medium)*

### Other essentials
- **Electrolytes + hydration:** low intake + any GI losses deplete sodium/potassium/magnesium →
  the "GLP-1 fatigue," dizziness, constipation people blame on the drug. ~½ your bodyweight (lb)
  in oz of water/day; sip, don't chug, when nauseous; supplement electrolytes. *(Medium)*
- **Fiber for GI sides:** ramp up **gradually** with water (too much too fast = bloating). *(Medium)*
- **⚠️ Gallstones:** rapid weight loss supersaturates bile → stones. Mitigate by **not chasing
  max weight-loss speed** (~0.5–1 kg/wk is the classic safe pace), keeping **some dietary fat**
  (triggers gallbladder emptying), and staying hydrated. In high-risk rapid loss, **ursodiol
  (ursodeoxycholic acid)** is proven to cut stone risk — a doctor's call. *(High)*
- **Body-comp check (DEXA or BIA) every 8–12 weeks** to confirm you're losing *fat*, not muscle. *(High)*

## 7. Suggested daily / weekly schedule (integration template, not a validated protocol)

**Daily**
- **On waking:** Semax (intranasal) → then your **highest-protein meal** (appetite best now).
  Hydrate + electrolytes.
- **Midday:** protein-first meal; **resistance training** here or late afternoon.
- **Afternoon:** protein snack (25–30 g); optional steps/light cardio. *(Last Semax by ~3–4 PM.)*
- **Dinner (earlier, protein-first, not heavily carby):** finish ≥2 h before bed.
- **Bedtime (fasted ≥2 h):** Tesamorelin SC; then protect **7–9 h sleep** (that's where the GH
  benefit and recovery actually happen).

**Weekly**
- **One fixed Retatrutide day** (e.g. Sunday). GI sides often peak 24–48 h later, so make that
  your lightest-training / easiest-protein day if you tend to get nauseous.
- Resistance training **3–4×/week**; Tesamorelin nightly; Semax mornings (or per cycle).
- Body-comp check every 8–12 weeks; labs per §8.

## 8. Bloodwork to monitor (the safety backbone)

Get a **baseline before/early**, then **~3 months**, then **6–12 months** (a doctor should
individualize intervals):

| Test | Why |
|---|---|
| **IGF-1** | Tesamorelin raises it — keep within age range, **don't push above it** (36–47% of trial patients overshot). Most important "don't overshoot" lab. |
| **Fasting glucose + HbA1c** | GH ↑ glucose, retatrutide ↓ it — watch the net. |
| **Lipase + amylase** | Pancreatitis screen (interpret *with* symptoms; >3× ULN + abdominal pain = stop). |
| **Comprehensive metabolic panel** | Kidney + liver. |
| **Lipid panel** | Both drugs affect lipids. |
| **TSH / free T4** | GH can lower free T4. |
| **CBC** | General safety. |
| **Resting HR + blood pressure** | Retatrutide raises HR ~5–8 bpm — track at home. |

## 9. 🚨 Red flags — stop and see a doctor

- **Severe, persistent abdominal pain radiating to the back (± vomiting)** → possible
  pancreatitis. Stop, seek care.
- **Right-upper-belly pain (esp. after fatty meals), fever, jaundice** → gallbladder attack.
- **Persistent vomiting / dehydration** (can trigger kidney injury).
- **Sustained racing/pounding heart, palpitations** beyond the expected modest HR rise.
- **Severe hypoglycemia** (shakes, confusion, sweating) — esp. if you ever add insulin/sulfonylureas.
- **Vision changes** (rapid glucose drops can transiently worsen retinopathy).
- **Neck lump, hoarseness, trouble swallowing** → thyroid warning (medullary thyroid cancer
  class concern).
- **Allergic reaction** — rash, swelling, trouble breathing.
- **Tesamorelin-specific:** significant swelling/joint pain/carpal-tunnel symptoms = likely
  dosing too high. *(All high confidence — from FDA labels/trials)*

## 10. Injection & reconstitution best practices

**Bacteriostatic water** (0.9% benzyl alcohol) is correct for multi-dose vials — the
preservative limits microbial growth across repeated draws (plain sterile water is single-use).
Discard ~28 days after first puncture (or sooner if the peptide degrades). *(High)*

**Mixing:**
- Alcohol-swab **both** stoppers, let dry ~15 s.
- Aim the water stream **down the glass wall**, not blasting the powder.
- **Swirl gently — never shake** (shaking/foaming denatures peptides).
- **Refrigerate (2–8 °C), protect from light** (amber vial or foil); **never freeze**
  reconstituted solution. Write the **reconstitution date + mg/unit** on the vial.
- **Toss if** cloudy, has floaters, or changes color — but note **some degradation is invisible**,
  so don't rely on looks alone (esp. fragile tesamorelin). *(High)*

**Injecting (subQ):**
- Sites: **abdomen** (avoid ~1 inch around the navel — most consistent absorption), thighs,
  flanks/love-handles, back of upper arms.
- **4–6 mm, 31–32 G** insulin needle; 90° angle (pinch a fold if you're lean).
- **Rotate sites** ~1 finger-width each time to avoid lipohypertrophy (lumpy fat that wrecks
  absorption). Never inject into lumps.
- **New sterile needle every time**, fresh needle into the vial each draw, **never share**,
  dispose in a sharps container. *(High)*

## 11. Sourcing & the honest bottom line

These are gray-market for you (retatrutide especially — it can't be legally compounded; anything
sold online is unregulated). Independent testing of research peptides has found **wrong/truncated
sequences, under- or over-dosing, zero active ingredient, heavy metals, and endotoxin/microbial
contamination.** A vendor's own "99% pure" certificate doesn't cover sterility or endotoxins.
*(High on the categorical risks)*

**If you continue regardless, harm-reduction priorities:**
1. **Get baseline + follow-up labs** (§8) — biggest single risk reducer.
2. **Insist on independent (third-party) Certificates of Analysis** — identity + purity *and*
   sterility/endotoxin — and stay skeptical of seller-run tests.
3. **Nail the retatrutide dose math** (§2) and write it on the vial.
4. **Don't change multiple things at once** — if you adjust a dose or add something, do it
   solo so you can attribute any reaction.
5. **Start low, go slow, keep it cold/dark/fresh, never share needles.**

> Final honest framing: the strongest evidence here is retatrutide's efficacy, the lean-mass/
> protein/training story, and the gallstone risk. The weakest is anything specific to running
> these three *together* (zero studies) and Semax's human benefits. A doctor + regular labs
> turn this from "flying blind" into something genuinely managed.
