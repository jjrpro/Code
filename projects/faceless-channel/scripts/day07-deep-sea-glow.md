# Day 7 — How do deep-sea creatures glow?

**Angle:** Bioluminescence visuals · **Ratio:** 9:16 · **Length:** ~70s

**HOOK (0–2s):**
In the deep ocean, most animals make their own light.

**BODY:**
Below a thousand meters, sunlight is gone — so life invented its own. It's called
bioluminescence: a chemical reaction inside the body that produces cold, living
light. Some fish dangle a glowing lure to draw prey closer. Some squid release a
blinding flash to escape. Others glow just to find a mate in total darkness. Down
there it isn't rare — scientists estimate the majority of deep-sea creatures can
produce light.

**PAYOFF:**
The darkest place on Earth is full of living stars.
