# Day 4 — The library that survived 2,000 years

**Angle:** History mystery · **Ratio:** 9:16 · **Length:** ~75s

**HOOK (0–2s):**
One library survived being buried by a volcano — and we're still reading it.

**BODY:**
When Vesuvius buried the town of Herculaneum in 79 AD, it carbonized an entire
library of scrolls — hundreds of them, turned to brittle black lumps. For
centuries they were unreadable; trying to unroll them destroyed them. Then
scientists scanned the charred scrolls with X-rays and trained AI to spot the
faint traces of ancient ink. Words began to appear — philosophy lost for two
thousand years, read without ever opening the scroll. Thousands more may still
lie buried at the site.

**PAYOFF:**
A volcano tried to erase these words. It accidentally preserved them.
