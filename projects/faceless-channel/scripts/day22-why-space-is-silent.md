# Day 22 — Why is space silent?

**Angle:** Vacuum + sound · **Ratio:** 9:16 · **Length:** ~70s

**HOOK (0–2s):**
In space, no one can hear you — because sound has nothing to travel through.

**BODY:**
Sound is a wave; it needs molecules to push against — air, water, something. Space
is a near-perfect vacuum, almost empty. So an explosion that would deafen you on
Earth makes no sound at all out there. Astronauts talk by radio, which is light,
not sound. There's one eerie exception: in the thin gas between galaxies,
telescopes detected pressure waves from a black hole — a single note so low it's
billions of times deeper than anything we can hear.

**PAYOFF:**
The loudest events in the universe happen in total silence.
