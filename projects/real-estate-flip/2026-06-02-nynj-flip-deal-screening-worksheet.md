## 0. What "middleman in the sale" legally means here

You are **wholesaling**: you put a property under contract, then assign that
contract to an end buyer for a fee — or do a quick double-close. In NY and NJ
this is legal **only if you stay on the right side of two lines:**

1. **You must hold a real equitable interest** (a signed purchase contract in
   your name). You are selling *your contract*, not the *property*. If you
   market the house itself without owning it or a license, that's unlicensed
   brokerage.
2. **You must disclose** that you are the contract holder and intend to assign,
   and (best practice) get the seller's written acknowledgment of the
   assignment.

**Distressed-seller laws — read before you ever contact a homeowner in default:**
- **NY:** Home Equity Theft Prevention Act (HETPA) + the Distressed Property
  Consultant rules (GBL Art. 36-A). Strict written contracts, no upfront fees,
  5-day cancellation, anti–equity-stripping provisions.
- **NJ:** Foreclosure Rescue Fraud Prevention Act. Mandatory disclosures,
  cancellation rights, criminal penalties for violations.
- Bottom line: buying a **listed** property through its agent = normal real
  estate. Knocking on a defaulting owner's door with a rescue pitch = heavily
  regulated. Know which one you're doing.

## 3. The deal math (run this on EVERY property)

```
ARV  = After-Repair Value (from SOLD comps, last 90 days, same area + beds/baths)
Max Allowable Offer (MAO) = (ARV × 0.70) − Repairs − Your Assignment Fee
```

- **Spread you keep** = (end buyer's price) − (your contract price). That's your
  assignment fee.
- At **$350k+ in NY/NJ**, dollar spreads are bigger but % margins are thin and
  transfer taxes bite — so:
  - **Comps must be sold, recent, and local.** One stretched comp kills it.
  - **Account for NY/NJ closing friction:** NYC has a Mansion Tax (1% at $1M+,
    tiered higher), NY/NJ transfer taxes, attorney-state closings (both NY and
    NJ are attorney-closing states — budget for it). These eat your buyer's
    margin, so they eat your assignment room.

## 5. Workflow to actually get your "20"

1. Run the §1 filters across the §2 counties → export every hit to a list.
2. Drop each into the §4 screen.
3. Keep the top 20 by spread.
4. Pull ARV comps before contacting anyone (Zillow "recently sold" + Redfin).
5. Line up cash/flipper end buyers **before** you tie up a contract — at
   $350k+ the buyer pool is shallower.
6. Offer at MAO with an inspection/assignment clause + your attorney.
