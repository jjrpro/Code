# Building Your Prospect List — the "5-mile-radius from 10312" part

You asked for "all businesses within 5 miles of 10312." There's no honest
shortcut that spits out a perfect list — but here's the exact, repeatable way
to build one in an afternoon. The goal: a spreadsheet of local businesses that
**have no website or a bad/outdated one**. Those are your buyers.

10312 covers Annadale, Eltingville, Huguenot, Arden Heights, Woodrow, Pleasant
Plains, Prince's Bay, Rossville. A 5-mile radius also pulls in Great Kills,
Tottenville, Charleston, and parts of New Springville.

## Step 1 — Pull the raw list (free, 30–45 min)

Use **Google Maps** (it's the source homeowners actually use):

1. Open Google Maps, search a category + area, e.g. `pizzeria near Annadale, Staten Island`.
2. Repeat for each category you want (list below).
3. For each result, the side panel shows whether they have a **"Website"**
   button. **No Website button = hot lead.** A 2010-looking site = warm lead.
4. Drop each into the tracker (`sales/prospect-tracker.csv`).

Other free sources to cross-check:
- **Yelp** — filter by neighborhood; many listings have no linked site.
- **Facebook** — tons of SI businesses run *only* a Facebook page. Perfect targets.
- **Instagram** — search location tags (Eltingville, Great Kills). If their
  "link in bio" is just a phone number, they need a site.
- **Drive down Amboy Rd, Hylan Blvd, Arthur Kill Rd** — the local commercial
  strips. Snap photos of storefronts, look them up later.

## Step 2 — Categories that convert best on SI

| Category | Why they buy |
|---|---|
| Pizzerias / delis / restaurants | Want online menu + orders; many only on FB |
| Contractors (roofing, siding, HVAC, plumbing) | High job value, need lead forms |
| Salons / barbers / nails / spa | Want bookings + service menu |
| Auto shops / detailing / towing | Want "call now" + inspection info |
| Landscapers / snow removal | Seasonal, run on word-of-mouth only |
| Cleaning services | Almost never have a site |
| Bakeries / caterers | Photos sell; great upgrade story |
| Dog groomers / pet services | Loyal niche, easy wins |

## Step 3 — Qualify before you pitch

Mark each lead **HOT / WARM / SKIP**:
- **HOT** — no website at all, active business, good reviews. Pitch first.
- **WARM** — has a site but it's ugly, not mobile-friendly, or broken. Show
  them yours next to theirs.
- **SKIP** — chain/franchise (corporate handles the site), already has a slick
  modern site, or out of business.

Quick test for WARM: open their site on your **phone**. If you have to pinch-zoom
or it looks like 2009 — that's your pitch.

## Step 4 — Track it

Log every lead in `prospect-tracker.csv` so you know who you've contacted and
when to follow up. Columns are already set up. Aim for **20–30 qualified leads**
before you start outreach — it's a numbers game.

## The legal / smart way to do this

- **Don't build full live sites for businesses without asking.** Putting up a
  site in someone's name that they never approved can cause trademark/impersonation
  headaches and burns the relationship. Instead: build a **1-screen mockup** (see
  the demos) with *their* name and colors, and pitch *that*. It's the same effort
  and it's flattering, not creepy.
- **Don't scrape or spam.** Manual list-building + personalized outreach converts
  far better and keeps you clear of CAN-SPAM / platform bans.
- **Honor opt-outs.** One "no thanks" = remove from list. Done.
