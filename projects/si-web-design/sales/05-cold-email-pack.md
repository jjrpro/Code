# Cold-Email Pack — Jaurx Web Design

Everything to run cold email for the local-website business: setup, templates,
follow-ups, and the rules so you don't land in spam (or trouble).

> ✏️ Swap every **[bracket]** for real info. Defaults: brand **Jaurx Web Design**,
> build **$400**, Care Plan **$400 + $40/mo**. Change in `03-pricing-and-delivery.md`.

---

## 0) Before you send a single email

**Use your new domain email, not a Gmail blast.**
- Send from something like **hello@jaurxwebdesign.com** (looks legit, lands in inbox).
- If you use Google Workspace/Zoho for it, turn on **SPF + DKIM** (the host walks you
  through it — it's a copy-paste DNS step). This is the #1 deliverability fix.

**Start slow.** 10–20 personalized emails/day for the first week, then ramp.
Blasting 200 cold emails day one from a new address = straight to spam.

**Personalize the first line every time.** One specific, true detail ("your
4.7★ reviews on Yelp", "loved the cannoli pics on your IG") beats any clever copy.

**One link only.** Link to the preview. More links = more spam signals.

---

## 1) The rules (CAN-SPAM — keep it clean & legal)

Every cold email MUST have:
1. **Honest subject + from line** — no "RE:" tricks, no fake names.
2. **Who you are** — your name + business in the signature.
3. **A real physical mailing address** — a PO box is fine. (Required by law.)
4. **An easy opt-out** — e.g. *"Not interested? Reply 'no thanks' and I won't email again."*
5. **Honor opt-outs fast** — remove them from your list, full stop.

Keep it B2B, low-volume, and genuinely useful (a free mockup) and you're well clear.

---

## 2) Subject lines (test a few)

Short, specific, no hype:
- `Quick website mockup for [Business Name] 👀`
- `Made [Business Name] a website — free to look`
- `[Owner Name], built you something for [Business Name]`
- `A new site for [Business Name]?`
- `Idea for [Business Name]'s online presence`
- `Saw [Business Name] on [Yelp/Instagram] — made you a mockup`

Avoid: ALL CAPS, "FREE!!!", "$$$", "act now", lots of emojis.

## 6) Follow-up sequence (most replies come from #2 and #3)

Space them out. Stop the moment they reply or opt out.

**Follow-up 1 — +3 days**
> Subject: re: [original subject]
>
> Hi [Owner Name], just bumping this up in case it got buried — did you get a
> chance to look at the mockup for [Business Name]? [preview link]
> No worries if it's not the right time. — [Your Name]

**Follow-up 2 — +5 days after that**
> Hi [Owner Name], last note from me on this — if a website's on your list this
> year, I'd love to help and can have [Business Name] live within a week.
> If not, no problem at all and I'll leave you to it. Either way, the mockup's
> yours: [preview link]. — [Your Name]

Two follow-ups, then stop. Mark them in the tracker and move on.

## 7) How to find business emails (since many don't list one)

In rough order of success:
1. **Their website contact page** — if they have a bad site, the email's often still there.
2. **Facebook page → About/Contact** — lots of SI businesses hide an email here.
3. **Google Business Profile** — sometimes shows an email or a "message" option.
4. **Instagram → "Email" button** on the profile (business accounts) — tap it.
5. **Just ask:** DM them "what's the best email to send something over?" — works great.
6. **Domain guesses** (only if they have a domain): info@ / hello@ / the owner's name.
   Verify before sending (a quick free email-verifier avoids bounces).

> ⚠️ Bounces hurt deliverability. If you're unsure an address is real, don't blast it.

Most of them (barber, deli, nail, bakery, boutique, tailor, dessert shop) are
**Instagram/phone/walk-in**, not email. For those, the Instagram DM script in
`02-outreach-scripts.md` is the play. **Cold email shines on a fresh list of
businesses that publish an email** — ask me to go pull one (home services,
contractors, and other email-listing SI businesses are the sweet spot).
