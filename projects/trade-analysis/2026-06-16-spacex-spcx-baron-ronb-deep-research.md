## ⚠️ The big problem with buying more of BOTH: you're not diversifying

This is the most important investment point, separate from the price issue.

**RONB is, to a large degree, a SpaceX/Musk fund.** Roughly:
- **~21.5% SpaceX**
- **~12% Tesla**
- **~5.4% xAI**
- → **~40% of RONB is Elon Musk-linked companies**, with SpaceX as its single
  largest holding.

So if you buy **more SPCX *and* more RONB**, you are **concentrating into
SpaceX**, not spreading risk. A chunk of your "two positions" is really **one
big SpaceX bet plus a Tesla/xAI bet.** That's the opposite of diversification.
If SpaceX stumbles, **both tiles fall together.**

## 📉 The bear case (why to be cautious)

- **Valuation looks stretched.** **Morningstar pegs SpaceX fair value around
  $780B — roughly 55% BELOW its ~$1.77T IPO valuation** — and called the shares
  "overvalued in almost any scenario." You'd be buying right after a hyped IPO
  that already popped ~19% on day one.
- **Post-IPO timing risk.** Buying immediately after the largest IPO ever, before
  insider **lockups expire**, is classic "buying the top" risk. Even Morningstar
  framed it as "smart investors wait."
- **RONB is expensive and extreme:** ~1.00% fee, <30 holdings, and **~27% in
  private companies** (SpaceX + xAI) that are **illiquid and valued by
  estimate, not by a live market** — Bloomberg noted Baron's stake "skirts" the
  SEC illiquid-asset cap. Brand new (Dec 2025), so little track record.
- **Concentration / key-man risk:** ~40% of RONB rides on Elon Musk companies
  and, indirectly, on Musk himself (political entanglements, headline risk).
- **Starship is still pre-operational.** Despite successes on Flights 10 and 11
  (late 2025), the critical **orbital refueling demo** — the gate for the NASA
  Artemis lunar lander — **was not yet done** as of mid-2026, and NASA
  **reopened the Artemis III lander contract to competition (Oct 2025)** citing
  SpaceX delays. The aggressive Mars/Moon timeline carries real execution risk.
- **Political / key-man risk is acute.** Musk led "DOGE" in early 2025, then had
  a **public rupture with Trump (~June 2025)** during which Trump **threatened to
  cancel SpaceX's government contracts** (no cancellation actually happened, but
  it showed the exposure). Musk's divided attention (Tesla, xAI, X, Neuralink)
  and supermajority voting control are structural risks.
- **Competition & capex:** Amazon "Leo" (ex-Kuiper), Blue Origin (New Glenn
  reached orbit Jan 2025), Rocket Lab, and Chinese state constellations are all
  investing heavily; Starship burns billions/year. Space is capital-intensive
  and cyclical.
- **Baron's own flagship fund is a cautionary tale on concentration:** Baron
  Partners Fund (BPTRX) is >60% Tesla+SpaceX, has a max drawdown around **-64%**,
  fell ~43–47% in 2022, and **Morningstar explicitly does NOT recommend it for
  most investors** due to "extreme" concentration. RONB is in the same family of
  risk.

## 🚨 Scam awareness (important given the SpaceX hype)

The SpaceX IPO triggered a wave of fraud. Real federal cases have specifically
named SpaceX:
- **SEC/DOJ v. Iakovou / Vika Ventures** — falsely advertised pre-IPO SpaceX
  shares; ~$5.9M stolen; 97-month prison sentence.
- **SEC v. StraightPath** — $410M pre-IPO fraud with SpaceX as a named "fund";
  founders imprisoned 2026.
- **Deepfake-Musk "stock/crypto giveaway" scams** around SpaceX launches.

Also note: **tokenized "SpaceX" products** (Robinhood EU tokens, xStocks,
Republic's rSpaceX) are **NOT actual share ownership** — they're debt/derivative
wrappers where you take the *issuer's* credit risk, with no voting and often no
US access. Regulators (SEC, ESMA) warn investors routinely mistake these for
real shares. **If your app's "SpaceX" is one of these, you don't own SpaceX —
you own a contract about SpaceX.**
